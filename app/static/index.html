                    Chapter 06

                    The build

                    182,293 records went in. Questions came out.

                    How it works

                    The data behind the music.

                    Extract, transform, load

                    From a zip to a database

                    Python opens Spotify's zip, cleans every record and loads six tables in order, so every key already exists when its row arrives.

                    Deduplication by title and artist

                    One song, many IDs

                    Spotify files one song as the single, the album and the deluxe. Same title and same artist means same song, so its listens add up.

                    Threshold filter

                    Thirty seconds or it didn't count

                    A play only counts at 30 seconds, Spotify's own rule. Shorter plays stay in the data as skips, which is how the skip rate exists.

                    Star schema

                    Every play points to four things

                    One table of plays in the middle, with the song, artist, day and session around it. Any question becomes a join away.

                    Sessionization, 30-minute gap

                    Where one session ends

                    If I stop for more than 30 minutes, the next song starts a new session. That's how 91,381 listens became 5,057 sessions.

                    Gaps and islands

                    Finding a streak

                    On days in a row, the date minus a row number stays the same. Same number, same streak: that's how SQL found my 21 days of one song.

                    Window function: ROW_NUMBER()

                    The 25th listen

                    Number every listen of a song in order. The day of listen number 25 is the day a song became an obsession.

                    Window function: RANK() by month

                    Who owned the month

                    Rank every artist inside each month by minutes and keep number one. Ariana won 43 of 53.

                    FastAPI, cache and a read-only login

                    Asked once, answered fast

                    Every chart is one SQL question behind one endpoint. It runs once, the answer is kept, and the login can read but never change a row.

                    What I learned

                    1. Cleaning decides the questions.Spotify files one song under several IDs: the single, the album, the deluxe. Until I merged them, my top songs were split across copies of themselves.
                    2. Not every play is a listen.Twice, a song kept looping while I wasn't listening. Left in, one of those loops would have been my biggest single day ever. The 30-second rule and a loop filter keep the data honest.
                    3. Having a field isn't a reason to keep it.The export tracks where and on what I listened. None of my questions needed it, so none of it was kept.
